# Business Insights & Recommendations

## 1. Executive Summary

The De Meridian Superstore Power BI dashboard provides a consolidated view of business performance across five major analytical dimensions:

* Overall business performance
* Customer performance
* Product performance
* Location performance
* Operational and shipping performance

The dashboard combines revenue, profit, quantity sold, profitability, customer activity, product performance, geographical performance, discounts, and shipping costs to provide a broader understanding of business operations.

The analysis is designed to help management move beyond individual figures and investigate **what is driving performance, where performance is strongest or weakest, and which operational factors may require attention**.

---

## 2. Overall Business Performance

The Overview page provides the primary business KPIs, including:

* Order activity
* Quantity Sold
* Total Revenue
* Total Profit
* Profit Margin

These indicators provide a high-level assessment of the organization's commercial performance.

The dashboard also compares **revenue and profit over time**, allowing changes in financial performance to be investigated across different periods.

### Management perspective

Revenue growth should not be evaluated independently. A business can generate increasing revenue while experiencing weaker profitability.

Therefore, monitoring **Revenue + Profit + Profit Margin together** provides a stronger basis for evaluating the quality of business growth.

---

## 3. Customer Performance

The Customer Performance page provides dedicated analysis of customer contribution.

The dashboard identifies:

* Top customers by profit
* Top customers by revenue
* Customer segments
* Customer performance over time
* Customer-level performance patterns

The use of separate revenue and profit rankings is particularly important because the customer generating the highest revenue is not necessarily the customer generating the highest profit.

### Management perspective

Customers should therefore be evaluated using multiple dimensions rather than revenue alone.

A customer contributing substantial revenue but comparatively lower profit may require further investigation into:

* Discount levels
* Product mix
* Order characteristics
* Shipping requirements
* Other costs associated with servicing the customer

---

## 4. Product Performance

The Product Performance page provides a detailed view of product-level performance.

The analysis examines:

* Product revenue
* Product profit
* Product trends
* Product rankings
* Discount patterns
* Differences in product profitability

This allows products to be classified conceptually into different performance groups:

**High Revenue / High Profit**
Products that contribute strongly to both sales and profitability.

**High Revenue / Low Profit**
Products generating sales but potentially requiring attention because profitability is comparatively weak.

**Low Revenue / High Profit**
Potential niche or high-margin products that may deserve stronger commercial attention.

**Low Revenue / Low Profit**
Products that may require review of pricing, demand, costs, or continued commercial relevance.

### Management perspective

Product decisions should therefore consider **profitability alongside sales volume**.

High sales volume alone does not necessarily indicate that a product is commercially optimal.

---

## 5. Discount Analysis

Discount is incorporated into the product and analytical views of the dashboard.

Discount analysis is important because discounts can increase sales activity while simultaneously reducing the amount of profit generated from individual transactions.

A useful management question is therefore:

> **Are higher discounts generating enough additional business to justify the reduction in profitability?**

Products or customers associated with relatively high discounts and weak profitability should be investigated further.

### Potential business action

Management could establish discount-monitoring thresholds and review products or customers where:

* Discount levels are unusually high
* Profit margins are weak
* Revenue contribution does not sufficiently compensate for the discount

---

## 6. Location Performance

The Location Performance page provides a geographical perspective of business performance.

The dashboard analyzes location-related performance using measures such as:

* Revenue
* Profit
* Average Revenue by Location
* Average Profit by Location
* Regional performance
* Location-level activity

The regional analysis allows management to compare performance between geographical markets.

### Management perspective

Differences between locations can indicate variations in:

* Customer demand
* Product preferences
* Revenue generation
* Profitability
* Commercial activity

High-performing locations can be studied for practices that may be transferable to weaker locations, while consistently weak locations may require deeper investigation.

---

## 7. Operational & Shipping Analysis

The Operational Analysis page focuses on the relationship between business performance and shipping activity.

The dashboard analyzes:

* Shipping Cost
* Shipping Cost as a percentage of Revenue
* Shipping Cost by Region
* Shipping Cost by Ship Mode
* Profit by Ship Mode
* Shipping trends over time
* Revenue in relation to shipping activity

This provides an important operational perspective because shipping expenses can directly affect profitability.

### Management perspective

A business may generate strong revenue but still experience margin pressure if shipping-related costs are high.

Therefore, **Shipping Cost as a percentage of Revenue** is an important operational KPI.

---

## 8. Ship Mode Analysis

The dashboard compares different shipping modes using financial and operational measures.

This enables management to investigate whether certain shipping methods are associated with:

* Higher shipping costs
* Higher profitability
* Greater business activity
* Different revenue contributions

The analysis should not automatically assume that the cheapest shipping method is the best option.

Instead, management should consider the relationship between:

**Shipping Cost → Revenue → Profit**

---

## 9. Cross-Dimensional Analysis

One of the strongest features of the dashboard is that the business can be investigated from multiple perspectives.

For example:

```text
Customer
   ↓
Products Purchased
   ↓
Discount Applied
   ↓
Shipping Method
   ↓
Shipping Cost
   ↓
Revenue
   ↓
Profit
```

This allows analysts to investigate relationships that would be difficult to identify from isolated spreadsheets or individual reports.

For example, a low-profit customer could potentially be investigated further by examining the products purchased, discounts received, and shipping characteristics associated with that customer's orders.

## 10. Key Business Questions Answered

The dashboard is designed to help answer questions such as:

### Overall Performance

* How is the business performing?
* What are the current revenue and profit levels?
* What is the overall profit margin?
* How does performance change over time?

### Customers

* Which customers generate the most revenue?
* Which customers generate the most profit?
* How do customer segments differ?
* Which customers may require further investigation?

### Products

* Which products perform best?
* Which products generate the highest profit?
* Which products have weaker profitability?
* How do discounts relate to product performance?

### Locations

* Which regions generate the strongest performance?
* How does profitability differ geographically?
* Which locations may require further investigation?

### Operations

* How much is being spent on shipping?
* What percentage of revenue is consumed by shipping?
* Which shipping modes have the highest shipping costs?
* How does shipping activity relate to profitability?

---

## 11. Recommended Business Actions

Based on the analytical framework of the dashboard, management can consider the following actions:

### 1. Monitor profitable growth

Track revenue and profit together rather than using revenue as the sole measure of growth.

### 2. Review high-discount transactions

Investigate situations where substantial discounts are associated with weak profitability.

### 3. Evaluate products by profitability

Avoid judging products purely by sales volume. Include profit and margin when making product decisions.

### 4. Segment customers by value

Identify customers that contribute strongly to revenue and/or profit and develop strategies around their value.

### 5. Investigate regional differences

Use location-level analysis to understand why certain markets outperform others.

### 6. Monitor shipping efficiency

Track shipping cost relative to revenue and investigate expensive shipping patterns.

### 7. Compare shipping modes

Evaluate shipping methods based on their combined effect on cost, revenue, and profitability rather than cost alone.

---

## 12. Analytical Limitation

The dashboard provides strong descriptive and diagnostic analysis, but the visuals alone should not be interpreted as proof of causation.

For example:

> A high discount and low profit occurring together does not automatically prove that the discount caused the low profit.

Additional analysis would be required to establish causality.

This distinction is important when using the dashboard for management decisions.

---

## 13. Overall Business Impact

The dashboard provides management with a centralized analytical environment where business performance can be examined from multiple dimensions.

Instead of relying solely on raw transactional records, decision-makers can investigate:

**What happened → Where it happened → Which customers/products were involved → What operational factors may be associated with the outcome.**

This transforms the underlying business data into a more accessible **Business Intelligence and decision-support solution**.
